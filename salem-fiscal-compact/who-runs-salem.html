A three-minute civics lesson

You've been blaming the wrong people for Salem's budget.

Salem is a council-manager city. That matters more than most residents realize.

City Manager

Runs day-to-day operations. Hires department heads. Builds the proposed budget every spring.

Mayor & City Council

Set policy. Write the rules the City runs under. Adopt the budget. Don't run departments.

Citizen Budget Committee

Council members plus an equal number of appointed residents. Reviews and votes on the budget before Council adopts it.

Voters

Approve levies, bonds, and charter amendments. Said no to the payroll tax in 2023.

So when people say "Council should run the City like a business," they're aiming at the wrong lever. Council doesn't run the City. Council writes the rules the City runs under. And right now those rules don't include the basics any business would insist on: don't fund payroll with one-time cash, don't approve a program without knowing what pays for it in year three, and don't give away assets you could lease.

That's why the Compact is written as rules, not cuts. Rules are what Council actually controls.

Where business judgment actually enters the process

The Citizen Budget Committee is where it happens. Residents appointed by Council sit alongside the councilors and vote on the budget before it's adopted. If you want financial discipline in the room, that's the room. Appointments are made by Council; the openings are posted on the City's website.

Why the shortfalls keep happening

The General Fund is mostly police, fire, and retirement costs driven by labor contracts and PERS rates. Those aren't discretionary. What is discretionary is everything Council adds on top — new programs, new positions, new grant commitments — and nothing in the current rules requires a named, recurring funding source before the vote. Rule 3 of the Compact, "name the money before you vote," is one procedural rule that would have prevented most of the last decade's shortfalls.

The short version

Manager builds it. Budget Committee reviews it. Council adopts it. Voters approve taxes. The Compact aims at the one step Council fully controls: the rules.
